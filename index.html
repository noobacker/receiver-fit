Is he good, and would he fit our QB?

Pick an NFL receiver. We show how well he gets away from defenders, then how often he would be open in time if he joined a different team.

2021 NFL tracking data, Weeks 1 to 8 · 47 receivers · 32 teams

The short answer: it depends on his quarterback's speed. Pittsburgh's quick-throwing QB is a worse fit for every one of the 47 receivers. Philadelphia and Baltimore fit best. When seven teams changed QB mid-season, the model got the direction right for 71% of receivers.

      A receiver is "open" when he has space from the nearest defender. The scores are the extra yards of space he gets compared with a typical player lined up in the same spot. Above 0 is better than typical.

      Man coverageOne defender follows him around the field.
      Zone coverageDefenders guard areas, and he has to find a gap.

      Part 2If he joined another team

      Quarterbacks throw at different speeds. A fast thrower needs receivers who get open early. We check how often he would already be open by the time that team's QB lets go. Click any team on the right to compare.

      This means "open in time", not "more passes thrown to him". In our data, being open early did not reliably lead to more targets.

      ProofWe tested it on real QB changes

      Seven teams switched quarterbacks mid-season. For 28 receivers we predicted how his "open in time" rate would change under the new QB, then compared with what really happened.
