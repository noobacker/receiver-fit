Is he good, and would he fit our quarterback?

One question in two parts, for 47 NFL receivers (2021 tracking data, Weeks 1 to 8). First: how well does he get open, and against which coverage? Then: how often would he be open before another team's QB throws?

Part 2

Would he fit a new quarterback?

His open-time curve is held against each team's dropback clock: the time its QB takes from snap to throw. The result is the share of that team's dropbacks on which he would already be open.

Does it work when the QB changes?

Seven teams changed quarterbacks in Weeks 1 to 8. For 28 receivers we learned the curve with QB A only, predicted the change with QB B from his dropback times alone, and compared with what happened.

Each dot is a receiver. Change in share of routes open before the throw, in percentage points. r = 0.52 overall (p = 0.004), r = 0.93 for 11 receivers with 50+ routes under both QBs. The model gets the direction right but understates the size, about 3 times too small.

Why the QB and not the offensive line?

We tested the idea that late-opening receivers are wasted behind a weak line. On real plays, late openers on weak-line teams got open, got targeted and caught the ball no worse than on strong-line teams. The QB's timing is the mismatch that shows up.

Limits. 8 weeks of data, about 40 to 70 man routes per player, so single scores have wide error. Part 2 is a grounded estimate, validated on 7 teams and 28 receivers, not a proven prediction of target share. "Open" means 3+ yards past the line and 3+ yards from every defender, at least 0.5 s after the snap.
